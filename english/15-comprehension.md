# English 15 — Comprehension (Prose and Poetry)

**Official line:** Comprehension: local, global, factual, critical/inferential — prose and poetry  
**Time:** 55 minutes + weekly practice  

---

## Today's goal

Know the **four question types** the syllabus names, and a method that works in the hall.

---

## 1. What comprehension tests

Not your general knowledge. **Only the given passage or poem** + what it clearly suggests.

---

## 2. The four official kinds

| Kind | Meaning | Typical stem |
|------|---------|--------------|
| **Local** | One line, one word, one sentence — the answer is **in a small place** | “In line 3, *bright* means…” “Who opened the door?” |
| **Global** | The **whole** text — main idea, title, tone, purpose | “The best title is…” “The passage is mainly about…” |
| **Factual** | Directly stated fact — you can **point** to the sentence | “How many students were there?” “When did they arrive?” |
| **Critical / inferential** | Not written in one line; you **read between the lines** — why, attitude, next step, moral | “The writer suggests that…” “We can infer that the boy was…” |

**Local + factual** often overlap (a fact in one line).  
**Global + inferential** often overlap (theme of the whole poem).

**Critical** also means: judge the writer’s view, irony, or whether a statement is opinion.

---

## 3. Prose method (8 minutes)

1. Read the **questions first** (know what to hunt).  
2. Read the passage once, fast.  
3. For each question, go back to the **exact lines**.  
4. Vocabulary: replace the word in the sentence; pick the option that still fits.  
5. Title: must cover the **whole**, not one example.  
6. Do not use outside knowledge (“I know Bengaluru has…”) if the passage does not say it.

**Trap options:** too narrow, too wide, opposite, extra fact not in the text.

---

## 4. Poetry method

A poem uses **images, sound, and feeling**. Questions may ask:

| Ask | Look for |
|-----|----------|
| Speaker / addressee | *I, we, O wind* |
| Theme | nature, childhood, patriotism, death, hope |
| Mood / tone | joyful, sad, angry, peaceful |
| Figure of speech | simile, metaphor, personification, alliteration… (file 8) |
| Sound | rhyme, rhythm, repetition |
| Inferential | What does the rose / road / night **stand for**? |

**Literal vs figurative:**  
*The child is a flower* — not botany; it means tender / growing.

Read the poem **twice**: once for story, once for pictures and sounds.

---

## 5. Mini prose (practise now)

*Ravi reached the lab early. The door was locked. He sat on the step and revised his English notes. When the teacher came, she smiled and said he would do well in the test because he had used the waiting time wisely.*

**Local / factual:** Where did Ravi sit? *(on the step)*  
**Global:** The passage is about using waiting time well.  
**Inferential:** The teacher thinks hard work matters.  
**Critical:** The writer’s attitude to Ravi is **approving**.

---

## 6. Mini poem lines

*The wind tapped like a tired man,  
And like a host, “Come in,”  
I boldly answered…*

- *tapped like a tired man* — **simile** + **personification**  
- Inferential: the wind is made to feel human and weary  
- Mood: quiet, slightly strange

## 7. MCQs (method)

**1.** A question whose answer is in one line is mainly:  
a) Global only  b) Local  c) Only poetry rhyme  d) Concord  

**2.** “The best title” is usually:  
a) Local  b) Global  c) Only a prefix  d) A modal  

**3.** Inferential means:  
a) Copied word for word only  b) Suggested, not directly stated  c) Always a date  d) Only antonyms  

**4.** In poetry, “the road” may be:  
a) Only tar  b) Also a symbol of life’s path  c) A tense  d) An ISP  

**5.** You should answer comprehension from:  
a) Your village story  b) The given text  c) Only Wikipedia  d) Only grammar rules  

### Answers

1-b, 2-b, 3-b, 4-b, 5-b
